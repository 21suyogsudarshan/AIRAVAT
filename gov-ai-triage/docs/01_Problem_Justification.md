# Case 07: Governance — Problem Selection Justification

## 1. Stakeholders & Situation Overview
- **Primary Stakeholders:** Municipal Complainants, Departmental Triage Officers (PWD, Water, Sanitation, Electricity, Health), Senior City Administrators[cite: 10].
- **Core Problem Space:** A municipal grievance intake ecosystem receiving high-volume complaints across 4+ channels (Portal, Phone, WhatsApp/Social, In-person) in English, Hindi, and code-mixed Hinglish[cite: 10].

## 2. Comparative Analysis of Problem Framings

### Framing A (Rejected): Citizen-Facing Conversational Chatbot & Tracking Interface
- **Focus:** Building a conversational frontend for citizens to register complaints and query status[cite: 10].
- **Why Rejected:** Framing A addresses a superficial symptom (vague "in process" status updates) without resolving backend routing delays or inter-departmental ticket ping-pong[cite: 10].

### Framing B (Selected Winner): Cross-Departmental Semantic Intake, Auditable Triage, and Urgency Engine
- **Focus:** Backend administrative triage—converting multi-channel, code-mixed citizen inputs into semantically deduplicated, multi-tagged, confidence-scored tickets with human-in-the-loop verification and public auditability[cite: 10].
- **Why Selected:**
  1. **Root Cause Targeting:** Case evidence shows 23% of complaints are reassigned at least once[cite: 10]. Framing B directly eliminates inter-departmental pass-along churn[cite: 10].
  2. **High Public Safety Impact:** Automated urgency scoring ensures high-risk hazards (e.g., live electrical wires, broken water mains) jump to the top of triage queues rather than getting lost in intake volume[cite: 10].
  3. **Strict Boundary Compliance:** Maintains mandatory human oversight—AI never closes or rejects tickets autonomously[cite: 10].
